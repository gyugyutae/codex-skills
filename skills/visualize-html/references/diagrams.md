# Draw the relationship, not decoration

Choose the visual form from the supplied meaning:

| Meaning | Visual form | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Change | Before/after panels | The same item keeps its label, color, and identity; its old and new locations are explicit. |
| Connection | Labeled nodes and directed edges | Every edge has supplied support and a clear meaning. |
| Comparison | Aligned parallel blocks | Compare on the same dimensions without inventing missing values. |

Use boxes for objects or containers and inner marks for their contents. These can represent teams, documents, components, or other concepts; they are not tied to software. Keep the encoding consistent within the drawing. Add a compact key only when needed to understand it.

In a transformation, show the resulting state, not just an arrow toward it. A moved item disappears from its original location; a split preserves the original contents across the new containers; consolidation shows the shared item and its remaining users. Show copying instead only when that is what the source describes. Do not mistake real compatibility connections for leftover duplicate implementation.

Separate transformation arrows from execution or reference arrows using distinct placement, labels, or line styles. A plain mention of A and B does not establish an A → B edge. Clarify important unknown relationships before drawing them.

Use source names when available. Neutral labels such as A/B can illustrate supplied abstract relationships; they must not invent a concrete case, number, or structure. Reuse requested original images instead of reconstructing their contents.

The audience should see what the objects are, how they connect, and what changes. A short caption explains that meaning; it does not add progress reports or commentary.
